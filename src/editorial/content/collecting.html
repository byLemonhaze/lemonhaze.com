Collecting

Bitcoin inscriptions are my primary medium. This is where the fullest expression of my practice lives, and where I place its greatest artistic value.

Some works are still images. Others unfold through code, interaction, or time. Collecting begins with the work itself: its image, its behaviour, and the record that identifies it.

What you collect

An inscription records digital content on Bitcoin. It has a specific inscription ID: the reference that lets you distinguish that work from another inscription or a saved copy of its image.

You can enjoy an image, save it, or display it without holding its inscription. Collecting the inscription means acquiring the particular digital artifact and its traceable history on Bitcoin. A screenshot or print does not transfer it.

The satoshi, the UTXO, and your wallet

A satoshi, or sat, is the smallest unit of bitcoin. Ordinal theory tracks individual sats as they move through transactions; an inscription is associated with a particular sat.

A UTXO means “unspent transaction output.” Think of it as a parcel of bitcoin that has not yet been spent. The inscribed sat is held within one of these outputs. Your wallet manages the keys that let you control and transfer it.

When you transfer the inscription, a Bitcoin transaction spends the old output and creates new ones. An inscription-aware wallet arranges that transaction to send the inscribed sat to the intended recipient. The UTXO changes; the inscription ID remains its reference.

Use a wallet that explicitly supports inscriptions and keeps them separate from bitcoin used for everyday payments. An ordinary payment can accidentally spend an inscribed sat, including as a transaction fee.

A first collection, one work at a time

1. Find the work. Start with Selected Work or explore All Works in the navigation. Open the artwork, spend time with it, and read its notes.

2. Check its identity. Follow the inscription link from this site. Compare the exact inscription ID with the listing you are considering. Check the series, edition size, and any parent inscription that establishes the collection relationship. A matching image or title alone is not enough.

3. Find availability. Supply & Marketplace brings together the collection record and marketplace links. Market Watch shows tracked secondary listings; availability and prices can change. For a direct inquiry, email collect@lemonhaze.com with the title or inscription link.

4. Receive and verify. Use your wallet’s inscription receiving instructions. Review the work, destination, price, and network fee before confirming. After confirmation, check that the inscription appears in your wallet. Keep your recovery phrase private; it is never needed for a collecting inquiry.

Live with the work

Open the original inscription viewer linked from the artwork page. For a still image, preserve its proportions. For a coded work, allow it to run: a thumbnail may show only one moment of it.

Controls and exports vary by artwork. Follow the instructions in its viewer. A saved image or recording is a way to display a moment; it does not create another inscription. Similarly, an output made with a Paint Engine does not automatically become a newly inscribed work.

In BEST BEFORE, activation and Bitcoin block time are part of the work’s behaviour. A recording preserves a particular experience, while the inscription continues according to its rules.
