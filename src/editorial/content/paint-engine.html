An evolving paint engine

The paint engine is a place to work through an idea: change its conditions, generate possibilities, keep what resonates, and return to the code.

Three inscribed milestones

These works trace the development of the engine across different versions. Each is a separate inscription and a point in an ongoing practice.

What I control, what I let vary

In Passe-Partout, the controls separate palette, aspect ratio, brush mode, texture, frame, and layout. “Generate” changes the overall starting point; “Iterate” makes a new layout while keeping the palette and seed. This lets a visual idea develop through a family of outputs.

DecisionIn the studio
Palette, ratio, brush, textureSet the visual conditions of the work.
Generate and iterateExplore compositions within or beyond those conditions.
Review and saveKeep outputs that resonate; continue refining the system.

For 1/1s and curated series, which is more in line with my personal interest and broader process I tend to keep the algo "wild" and often generate many outputs (within the same idea) from which I pick the one I resonate with on a given day. This allows me to keep moving and continuously explore different ideas, as well as improving and changing specific aspects of my base algo and add/remove & craft new small functions, as I see fit. This approach is better suited for my quest to define this vague idea that drives me, and so I continuously iterate and evolve with it - and only from time to time I stop to inscribe something Either because I have something to say, something to document or I reached a fictive milestone where I feel satisfied (for a short moment) with the work that is in front of me.
